# Fleet and reservations pilot

Status: proposed experiment, not an adopted policy or live booking system. November 8, 2026.

The founder clarified that the group will be a very exclusive, trusted internal circle sharing interesting third cars. Start with self-service member handovers and a shared calendar; use the more structured allocation proposal below only if actual contention calls for it. A short readiness/mileage/issue note should keep the next friend informed without imposing a commercial rental ritual. Appoint service coordination, not necessarily paid custody. These ceilings allocate a hypothetical $245,000; they are not current valuations, expected deal prices, or evidence suitable cars exist at these amounts. Taxes, inspections, setup work, and reserves are separate. The unconfirmed “K12s” idea is not mapped to a model.

Require each candidate dossier to include independent inspection, title/lien and history checks, California registration/emissions feasibility, service records and catch-up work, insurer acceptance, service provider and parts availability, a realistic all-in budget, and sale/liquidity risks. Confirm vehicle-specific California requirements before purchase; no blanket exemption is assumed. Compare current actual cars and transactions only when acquisition work begins.

Assess collection diversity by driving experience, not badge count. Two cheap-to-run popular cars may deliver more useful access than a second expensive car used rarely. A model-specific mechanic and an independent PPI matter more than a tempting advertised price.

## Why capacity has to be per car

For illustration: four cars × 52 weekend blocks = 208 gross blocks; at assumed 20% unavailability, about 166 remain. A single sought-after 911 contributes only about 42 of them. Eight members would average roughly five peak weekends in that particular car annually; ten average roughly four. Actual downtime and demand may differ substantially. Do not sell the aggregate as a promise that everyone gets their first choice.

## Proposed 90-day manual trial

Use a shared calendar plus a ledger with pseudonymous member IDs. Only a human-approved pilot and actual insurer-approved drivers can create live reservations. The founder and eventual members must adopt the rules before use.

1. **Peak allocation:** define a weekend block as Friday noon to Monday 10am in America/Los_Angeles. Each quarter members submit ranked requests for peak blocks. Publish the allocation before opening the remaining inventory.
2. **Rotating draft:** establish a recorded initial order by draw; allocate one request per member per pass using a snake order within each draft. Rotate the starting member next quarter so nobody owns permanent first choice. Respect car availability, approved-driver restrictions, and maintenance holds first.
3. **Concentration cap:** propose no more than one advance peak booking in the same car per member per quarter during the draft. Cap all advance peak bookings at three per member per quarter. After the draft, allow only one additional active advance reservation per member at a time; already allocated draft bookings do not count toward that additional-booking limit. Review against real demand; do not sell these caps as entitlements.
4. **Unused inventory:** open unallocated slots after the draft; within seven days of pickup, release concentration/advance caps for otherwise idle cars. Do not cancel an existing booking to make room for a different member. Record short-notice use separately from advance access so high usage does not automatically mean unfairness.
5. **Weekdays and length:** weekdays use simple first-come booking within a proposed 30-day window; ordinary non-peak trips max three consecutive days. Longer trips and holiday blocks need an explicitly published allocation rule, not founder favoritism. No member may hold overlapping car bookings.
6. **Cancellation:** more than seven days ahead, release without a fairness debit. Later cancellations count as used peak access if nobody takes the slot, with exceptions for club-caused downtime and documented emergencies handled consistently. No monetary penalties until a fee schedule is adopted.
7. **Downtime:** a safety or service hold overrides access. Offer an available alternative or restore the member's booking priority; no guaranteed replacement. Club-caused cancellations do not consume a member's allotment. Record the reason and impact.

All calendar edits must be reflected in the ledger, with a unique booking ID and last-updated time. Resolve policy changes with members before applying them to live reservations.

Initial fees remain open. Equal peak-access rights can coexist with mileage-based wear contributions, per-day fees, or a premium car charge, but test whether these change tax/insurance classification and member demand. Reconcile any usage charges with the model and do not count gross revenue as gap-closing cash without associated costs/taxes.

## Measures that justify changing the rules

Track by car and member: requested and granted peak blocks, preference rank satisfied, wait time, cancellations, available versus unavailable days, completed days/miles, short-notice use, complaints, maintenance cost, and incidents. Calculate fulfillment using actual requests, including denied ones, rather than bookings alone. If one car is oversubscribed while another sits, revisit caps, fleet mix, or pricing at a member review. Never buy another car merely because aggregate utilization looks high.

## Physical operating procedures

**Before access:** record insurer approval and license-verification status securely; provide car-specific familiarization, operating notes, roadside procedure, and signed final member/use terms. Sensitive documents live in a secure record system, not this repo.

**Checkout:** confirm no service hold, driver authorization, booking, tire/fluid/warning-light check, odometer/fuel, condition photos, keys, documents, expected return time, and emergency contact. Use a clear vehicle status: available, reserved, checked out, inspection hold, maintenance, or unavailable.

**Return:** record time/mileage/fuel/photos, damage or warnings, lost items, cleanliness, key custody, and next-booking readiness. A damage dispute must not leave an unsafe car bookable. A physical custodian performs this check; an agent can track the record but cannot attest to inspecting a vehicle.

**Incident:** prioritize immediate safety and appropriate emergency help; use the actual insurer's claims/roadside instructions and founder contact. Do not invent a claims number. Preserve observations/photos and notify the responsible person; no promise about fault, reimbursement, coverage, or deductible allocation. A qualified person clears a safety hold after documented inspection/repair.

**Maintenance:** track calendar and mileage service due dates, inspection findings, tires/brakes/fluids, repair approvals, invoices, service history, and next action. Reserve service time before accepting conflicting bookings. Define founder-approved routine spend and emergency limits before delegating spending.

## Initial ledger fields

Booking: ID, pseudonymous member ID, vehicle ID, requested start/end/timezone, peak flag, preference rank, status, allocation method, cancellation reason, actual miles, and cost reference. Vehicle: ID, role, secure record reference, availability/hold, due date/mileage, custodian, and current action. No real VINs, home addresses, or license details are needed for formation planning.
