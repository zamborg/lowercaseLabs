# Three possible shared third garages

November 8, 2026 · Spitballing with price evidence, not an acquisition recommendation. All amounts USD.

The brief is a very exclusive, trusted internal group around Portola Valley sharing interesting third cars and scarce garage space. A useful electric truck belongs in that idea just as much as a classic sports car. Build for variety and actual desire to borrow the cars. The exotic is an optional flavor, not the club's reason to exist.

My starting preference is **A: Miata + air-cooled 911 + electric Silverado + manual V12 BMW**. Each slot delivers something meaningfully different. It also leaves more room to keep the collection healthy than filling the entire garage with expensive headline cars.

## Your V12 manual 7 Series idea

It can be real, with an important specification detail. BMW's [E38 750i archive](https://www.bmwgroup-classic.com/en/models/bmw-classics/product-description-page.ad-205-1.bmw-750i-e38.html) describes the V12 car with a five-speed automatic. The manual E38 V12 example found here is a conversion: [a six-speed-swapped 2001 750iL sold for $37,000 on February 22, 2023](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2001-bmw-750il-21/). That is an older special-build result, not a current market price or proof of present availability.

I would put three interpretations on the menu:

- **Keep V12 + manual:** start with an E31 850i factory six-speed, subject to verifying each car's actual build/specification. It captures much of the eccentric grand-touring appeal without needing to commission a conversion.
- **Keep the E38 shape + V12:** explore an automatic 750iL. Recent examples reported [sold at $28,250 in May 2026](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2001-bmw-750il-11-2/) and [sold at $31,500 in March 2026](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2001-bmw-750il-35/); another [sold for $12,750 in August 2026 after seller-reported roughly $50k of service](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2001-bmw-750il-37/). That last result is a vivid reminder to distinguish purchase price from restoration spending, not an expected annual bill.
- **Keep the exact dream:** wait for a well-documented manual V12 E38 conversion and inspect the workmanship, diagnostics, clutch/driveline, registration and insurance fit. Do not use the lone 2023 result to budget a fresh build.

## How I would choose between the garages

**A is the clearest expression of the idea.** You could borrow the Miata for a quick drive, the 911 for an analog weekend, the truck when it is genuinely useful, and the V12 coupe for a different kind of trip. Buying fewer trophies also gives members more freedom to actually use the cars. The old V12 is the quirky slot; proceed only when there is a shop and a strong specific car behind it.

**B is tempting because used purchase prices make a McLaren look surprisingly reachable.** The cheap modified $68k result is not a reason to shop at the bottom. Prefer documented condition and a specialist inspection over a tune or a claim of low mileage. Budget and accept downtime as part of the choice. The early 12C experience is a different proposition from a manual Ferrari.

**C is my preferred exotic direction if the group wants the shifter and the occasion.** That is taste, not a forecast of reliability or investment return. The factory-manual specification is deliberate; don't substitute an F1-equipped or converted car without adjusting the price comparison and expectations.

For the truck slot, confirm the garage fits the vehicle and allows charging before settling on the Silverado. Chevrolet's [2024 launch specifications](https://news.chevrolet.com/newsroom.detail.html/Pages/news/us/en/2022/jan/ces/0105-2024-silverado.html) list 233.1 inches overall length—about 19 ft 5 in. Battery configuration, remaining warranty, charging arrangements and exact equipment need VIN-level verification. The choice should depend on whether members actually want utility, not just whether an EV truck is an amusing idea.

For a trusted group, I would initially keep the reservation workflow just as simple: shared calendar, keys, mileage and any issues for the next person. Bring in rotating priority only when the group has repeated conflicts. Let the cars and friendships determine the useful amount of structure.
