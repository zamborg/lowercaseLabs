# Initial COO briefing

November 8, 2026 · Formation research · All operating rules and budgets are proposals.

Four-car pilot baseline: the November 8 founder update set working dues at $400–$500/month and prioritized AutoKeep Menlo Park for a storage negotiation. The comparisons below retain the original dues examples as history; the [generated pilot scenarios](../finance/scenarios.md) use $400/$450/$500, and the [storage brief](storage-plan.md) tests member-led operations and a potential group rate. The November 17 [founder handoff](founder-handoff.md) considers a separate six-car, twelve-member proposal. Neither case has quoted operating costs.

## Recommendation

Develop a four-car, eight-member pilot as the planning case, with ten members as a financial sensitivity. Before presenting firm membership terms, prove three things together: the intended shared use is insurable, the operating budget works, and the members accept realistic access and exit rules. Recruit for fit and discovery now; accept capital only after the formation and launch conditions are defined.

The concept's appeal is a curated collection and trusted community. The founder subsequently clarified a very exclusive internal group sharing interesting third cars around Portola Valley. Plan simple member-led handovers and light coordination; a staffed concierge service is not assumed. The paid-management base budget remains a sensitivity, not an adopted service model.

## What deserves attention today

1. **Insurability of the actual arrangement.** Prepare one factual description of ownership, dues, proposed usage fees, drivers, mileage, storage, and allowed use. Have a specialist broker identify a viable policy structure before committing to cars. A product labeled “car club insurance” may cover events rather than members driving a shared fleet: Hagerty's business overview describes its club product in event terms. Its collector eligibility guidance also excludes commercial-use vehicles. Neither page proves PBCC qualifies or fails to qualify. [Hagerty business overview](https://www.hagerty.com/insurance/business-insurance), [collector eligibility](https://www.hagertyagent.com/insurance/eligibility-guidelines).
2. **Recurring affordability.** Eight people at $300/month produce $2,400/month. AutoKeep lists $500/month per car for multiple indoor cars, or $2,000 for four, before shared-access terms are negotiated. That leaves $400 for everything else. Storage is a concrete early quote request, not a minor line item. [AutoKeep advertised pricing](https://www.theautokeep.com/).
3. **The rules for money and exits.** Determine what each $50,000 buys, how fees reduce collection equity, who can approve further calls, and what happens when someone wants out. Shared capital is not automatically a refundable deposit. Resolve this with formation counsel and the accountant before the recruiting pitch becomes an offer.
4. **Real access to the headline car.** Four cars do not mean four equally desired experiences. One 911 still offers only one car's weekends. Test this with prospective members before buying the fleet.
5. **Lightweight physical coordination.** In this trusted internal group, members can handle their own pickup/return checks and key handovers, with a rotating or named person coordinating service. Start with a calendar, keys, mileage and issue notes, and someone to call. Price paid support only for work the group wants to outsource. Avoid turning an occasional shared third car into a rental-counter experience.

## Capital buys the collection; dues keep it usable

Eight $50,000 contributions give $400,000; ten give $500,000. These are gross contributions, not a vehicle shopping allowance.

The [model](../finance/scenarios.md) uses a hypothetical $245,000 four-car purchase allocation, plus $22,050 acquisition tax at an assumed 9%, $4,000 inspections/transport, $20,000 initial mechanical work, $10,000 formation/setup, and a $10,000 initial management fee. That leaves $88,950 cash in the eight-member case. These are budget inputs, not researched vehicle prices or vendor quotes; the tax treatment and rate require confirmation.

Illustrative annual operating funding is **$82,400**: storage, insurance, maintenance, administration, management, contingency, the California LLC annual tax placeholder, and repair-reserve contributions. The insurance input is particularly uncertain. At eight members this requires about **$858/member/month**, before fuel paid by drivers, depreciation, capital opportunity cost, major losses, and any usage taxes. At ten members it is about **$687**. If an LLC is selected, FTB currently states an $800 annual tax; the model does not assume PBCC has formed one. [California FTB](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/limited-liability-company/index.html).

| Members | Dues/member/month | Annual dues | Annual operating funding gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | $300 | $28,800 | $53,600 |
| 8 | $400 | $38,400 | $44,000 |
| 10 | $300 | $36,000 | $46,400 |
| 10 | $400 | $48,000 | $34,400 |

This does not establish that the concept is unaffordable; it establishes that the target dues do not yet fund the proposed service. Options are cheaper storage, less paid operating work, a smaller/less costly fleet, higher dues, usage charges, or an explicit annual assessment. Each changes the member experience. More members increase funds but reduce access per person. Spending capital on a recurring deficit shrinks members' equity and is not a sustainable substitute for an operating plan.

For scale, closing a $44,000 gap with 400 paid driving days requires $110/day **net** of any additional cost and tax. Those 400 days are an illustration, not forecast demand, and costs already in the model must not be counted twice. Measure expected usage before choosing a fee schedule.

Proposed launch cash floor: six months of operating expenses, excluding reserve contributions, plus $25,000 for major repairs/deductibles: $63,200 in the base case. Keeping that floor at launch still leaves only $25,750 above it with eight members. At $300 dues, the projected net funding draw reaches the floor in about six months. The floor is a policy to discuss; it is not an actuarial estimate of maximum loss.

## Fleet and access

Buy roles, then choose actual cars: an approachable lightweight roadster, a second distinct sports-car experience, a classic anchor such as a 911, and a contrasting touring/utility car. A Miata is a founder-stated interest; exact generation, condition, and acquisition cost remain open. Keep the fourth slot flexible until member demand and service coverage are known. “K12s” remains unconfirmed.

The subsequent [sample fleets](sample-fleets.md) explore the founder's more specific direction: Miata, air-cooled 911, electric pickup, and a V12 grand tourer or optional exotic. These researched brainstorming cases do not replace the initial financial base case.

Use a quarterly rotating priority process for peak weekends, a per-car peak-booking cap, and open short-notice access to unused inventory. Start with a calendar and ledger. Do not build custom booking software before the group has tested the rules. The [pilot policy](fleet-and-reservations.md) includes cancellation, downtime, and damage handling.

As a capacity illustration, one popular car has 52 weekend blocks per year. Assuming 20% unavailable, that is about 42, or roughly five per member at eight members and four at ten. This is a planning ceiling, not a guarantee. Aggregate fleet capacity hides this bottleneck.

## The path to a credible recruiting tour

Work at the pace evidence supports; these are phases, not deadlines.

| Phase | Work | Evidence needed to advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | Founder interviews 4–6 likely members; COO prepares broker, garage, and shop inquiries | Actual use patterns, travel radius, appetite for fees, and major objections recorded |
| Feasibility | Broker, formation counsel, accountant, storage tours, service proposals | Written insurance indication/conditions, two storage comparisons, a service approach, and a funded operating case |
| Formation | Decide economics and governance; advisers prepare documents | Ownership/exit rules, clear fee disclosures, capital process, banking/bookkeeping setup, and launch conditions |
| Commitment | Founder takes a consistent proposal to selected people | Sufficient signed commitments under adviser-prepared terms; define any minimum cohort and refund conditions |
| Acquisition | Inspect actual cars, agree terms, bind insurance and storage in a coordinated sequence | VIN-specific coverage, title/tax checks, inspections, reserves intact, approved drivers and procedures |
| Pilot | Onboard, run manual reservations, review after 90 days | Reliable handovers, actual cost per mile/day, booking access and satisfaction; policy changes recorded |

My immediate operating work is preparing those conversations, turning responses into evidence, and keeping one clear list of decisions.
